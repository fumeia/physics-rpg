Question 2 [Rays in C2 ]:                                     
   Consider a quantum system with Hilbert space H = C2 and let |1⟩ , |2⟩                  be an
orthonormal basis of H.
 (i) Suppose we are given two non-zero state vectors |Ψ⟩ , |Ψ′ ⟩ ∈ H such that |Ψ′ ⟩ = µ |Ψ⟩
     for some non-zero µ ∈ C. Is there any observable difference between these two state
     vectors? For instance, what is the probability of observing a certain eigenvalue of
     some observable without assuming that state vectors have unit norm?
 (ii) Show that almost any state of the system can be represented by a vector of the
      form |1⟩ + z |2⟩ for some number z ∈ C. Which states cannot be represented in that
      form? How many of them are there? Do different values of z correspond to different
      states?
(iii) Think of the number z ∈ C as a complex coordinate. What state do you think we
      should assign to the limit |z| → +∞ of |1⟩ + z |2⟩?
(iv) Using the previous step, can you recognize the space of distinct states of this system
     as being (topologically) equivalent to some simple geometric object?
     Hint: Think of the space of physically inequivalent states as an extended complex
     plane C ∪ {point at infinity}, and imagine being able to stretch and deform the
     extended complex plane at will.
