     (b) The Lie algebra su(3) contains multiple smaller su(2) subalgebras. In the
         context of quantum mechanics, su(2) is well-known as the algebra describing
         angular momentum (eα ↔ J+ , fα ↔ J− , and hα ↔ 2Jz ).
         When we restrict our attention purely to one of these su(2) subalgebras, the
         states of the full system must still behave as valid states for this smaller algebra.
         A fundamental result of standard angular momentum theory is that for any
         finite-dimensional irreducible representation, the eigenvalues of the diagonal
         operator (analogous to 2Jz ) must be integers.
          Consequently, since the highest weight vector must be a valid state for all these
          internal su(2) subalgebras simultaneously, its eigenvalues λ(hα ) with respect
          to these diagonal operators must all be integers.
