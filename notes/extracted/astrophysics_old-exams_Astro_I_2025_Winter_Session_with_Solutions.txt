3. (3 points) In empty space, the specific intensity along a ray path remains constant be-
   cause:
         □ The energy density of radiation is conserved along the ray.
         ■ Both the received flux and the solid angle occupied by the source decrease as
           the inverse square of distance.
         □ The radiation field is isotropic in empty space.
         □ The flux of the radiation is zero when averaged over all directions.


    Solution: Since both the received intensity and the corresponding solid angle scale
    in the same way with distance, their ratio (which defines the specific intensity) re-
    mains unchanged. Therefore, along a ray path in empty space (i.e. in the absence of
    absorption and emission), the specific intensity is conserved.

7. (3 points) The 21 cm spectral line is one of the most important tools for mapping the
   structure of the Milky Way. What is the physical origin of this emission line?
         □ The recombination of an electron with a proton in ionized Hydrogen regions.
         □ Rotational transitions of Carbon monoxide molecules in dense clouds.
         ■ The hyperfine splitting of the ground state of neutral Hydrogen.
         □ Synchrotron radiation emitted by cosmic rays spiralling in the Galactic mag-
           netic field.


    Solution: The 21 cm line originates from the hyperfine splitting of the ground state
    of neutral hydrogen (HI). In the ground state of a hydrogen atom, the magnetic
    moments of the proton and the electron can be either aligned (parallel) or anti-
    aligned (anti-parallel). The energy difference between these two states is very small
    (∆E ≈ 5.9 × 10−6 eV). When the electron flips from the higher energy state (aligned
    spins) to the lower energy state (anti-aligned spins), a photon with a wavelength of
    21 cm (frequency ν ≈ 1420 MHz) is emitted.


8. (3 points) Stars in the Milky Way and similar galaxies are broadly categorized into
   two populations based on their age, location, and chemical composition. Which of the
   following statements best describes Population II stars?
         □ They are young, metal-rich stars located primarily in the galactic disk.
         ■ They are old, metal-poor stars located primarily in the galactic halo and bulge.
         □ They are stars currently forming within dense molecular clouds in the spiral
           arms.
         □ They are exclusively white dwarfs.
    Solution: Milky Way-like galaxies contain two main stellar populations:

        • Population I: These are young, bright, and metal-rich stars found in the disk.
          They are continuously formed from interstellar gas that has been enriched with
          metals by previous generations of stars.

        • Population II: These are old, metal-poor stars found in the halo and bulge.
          They were formed early in the galaxy’s history, before the interstellar gas was
          significantly enriched with metals.


9. (3 points) In the context of stellar dynamics, galaxies (with N ≈ 1011 stars) are often
   described as collisionless systems. What is the primary physical reason for this classifi-
   cation?
          □ The physical radius of stars is large enough compared to the distance between
            stars that close encounters are frequent.
          ■ The time required for gravitational encounters to significantly change the star’s
            velocities is much longer than the age of the Universe.
          □ The dark matter halo suppresses the random motions of stars, preventing them
            from interacting.
          □ The stars all have coherent circular velocities and thus remain equidistant from
            one another.


    Solution: In stellar dynamics, a system is said to be collisionless if the gravitational
    force acting on a star is determined by the smooth, mean potential of the entire
    system rather than by individual collisions from nearby stars. The timescale for
    these individual collisions to become significant is the relaxation time trelax . For a
    galaxy with N ≈ 1011 stars, trelax is on the order of 1015 years, which is orders of
    magnitude longer than the age of the Universe (∼ 1.4 × 1010 years). Therefore, stars
    in a galaxy effectively never “feel” the graininess of the potential, and the system is
    collisionless.

15. (3 points) If measurements indicate that the total density of the universe is greater
    than the critical density (i.e., Ωtot = ρtot /ρcrit > 1), what is the spatial geometry of the
    universe?
           □ Flat (k = 0)
           □ Open / Hyperbolic (k = −1)
           ■ Closed / Spherical (k = +1)
           □ Cylindrical


      Solution: The geometry of the universe depends on the total energy density relative
      to the critical density:

         • If Ωtot = 1 (ρtot = ρcrit ), the universe is Flat (k = 0).

         • If Ωtot < 1 (ρtot < ρcrit ), the universe is Open (k = −1).

         • If Ωtot > 1 (ρtot > ρcrit ), the universe is Closed (k = +1).
