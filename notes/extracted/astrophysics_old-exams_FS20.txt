Prof: Very goood. Now next topic: what can you tell me about the characteristics of spiral and
elliptical galaxies ?
Me: Spiral galaxies have a central bulge and the disk with spirals around it, elliptical galaxies on
the other hand have an elliptical shape. Spiral galaxies counter balance the gravitational pull with a
rotational motion in the disk and random motions in the bulge, while in elliptical galaxies we only find
random motions. Moreover, in spiral galaxies we find ISM and star formation is happening, we can
therefore find younger stars in spiral galaxies than in elliptical galaxies, where there is no ISM and the
stars are quite old.

Prof: Ok, we still have some time, so can you tell me in detail the thermal history of the universe?
Me: Here I basically repeat section 8.8 of the notes Quark epoch: time <10 6 s, the universe is a
quark-gluon plasma with energies above GeV. The energy is too high to form hadrons. The photon
density and quark density are comparable.
Hadron epoch (hadrons - particles held together by the strong force): time 10 6 to 1 s. During this
epoch quarks are bound into hadrons, photon energy smaller than GeV. Hadrons and anti-hadrons
eliminate each other (annihilation). There is a small asymmetry between matter and anti-matter, for
this reason today we see much more matter and antimatter. Still today: n ⇡ 10 8 nb . At the end of
the hadron area there are remaining stable particles: p, n, e, e+, neutrinos, and dark matter. Neutrinos
decouple, because of the low density of the universe they do not interact with “matter” anymore, so
there must be a neutrino background in the Universe.
Lepton epoch: time 1 s – 10 s, leptons dominated the “interacting” matter of the Universe. Electrons
