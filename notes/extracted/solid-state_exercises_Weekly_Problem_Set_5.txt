Exercise 1: Deriving the density of states for the linear
dispersion relation of the Debye model
In the lecture, we found that the linear dispersion considered in the Debye model yields
a density of states g(ω) ∝ ω 2 (in three dimensions). In this exercise, we will practice this
important derivation again and extend it to 1D and 2D.

  a) Write down the dispersion relation of the vibrational modes in the Debye model.

  b) Assume periodic boundary conditions. What is the distance between nearest-
     neighbour points in k-space? What is the density of k-points in 1, 2, and 3 di-
     mensions? Assume the system size is L for 1 dimension, L × L for 2 dimensions,
     and L × L × L for 3 dimensions.

  c) Express the number of states between frequencies 0 < ω < ω0 as an integral over
     k-space. Do so for 1D, 2D and 3D. Do not forget the possible polarizations: assume
     that in d dimensions there are pd polarizations.

  d) Transform these integrals into integrals over frequency for 1D, 2D and 3D using the
     dispersion relation. Indicate the integral boundaries. Extract the density of states.




Exercise 2: Debye model in 2D
Consider a solid material that is thin enough that phonons may only propagate in two
dimensions. Here we will consider the isotropic Debye model in two dimensions.

  a) Formulate an integral expression for the energy stored in the vibrational modes of
     a two-dimensional Debye solid as a function of T .

  b) Calculate the heat capacity in the high T limit.

  c) At low T , show that CV = KT n . Find n. Express K as an indefinite integral
     (similarly to what was done during the lecture).
