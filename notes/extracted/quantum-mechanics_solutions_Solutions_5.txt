Question 2 [Rays in C2 ]:                                     
   Consider a quantum system with Hilbert space H = C2 and let |1⟩ , |2⟩                    be an or-
thonormal basis of H.
 (i) Suppose we are given two non-zero state vectors |Ψ⟩ , |Ψ′ ⟩ ∈ H such that |Ψ′ ⟩ = µ |Ψ⟩
     for some non-zero µ ∈ C. Is there any observable difference between these two state
     vectors? For instance, what is the probability of observing a certain eigenvalue of
     some observable without assuming that state vectors have unit norm?
 (ii) Show that almost any state of the system can be represented by a vector of the form
      |1⟩ + z |2⟩ for some number z ∈ C. Which states cannot be represented in that form?
      How many of them are there? Do different values of z correspond to different states?
(iii) Think of the number z ∈ C as a complex coordinate. What state do you think we
      should assign to the limit |z| → +∞ of |1⟩ + z |2⟩?
(iv) Using the previous step, can you recognize the space of distinct states of this system
     as being (topologically) equivalent to some simple geometric object?
     Hint: Think of the space of physically inequivalent states as an extended complex
     plane C ∪ {point at infinity}, and imagine being able to stretch and deform the ex-
     tended complex plane at will.
Solution:
  (i) The two state vectors are proportional, so there is no difference between them.
      Indeed, if some (non-zero) vector |Φ⟩ is an eigenvector of some observable with some
      isolated non-degenerate eigenvalue, the probability of observing that eigenvalue is
                                                 |⟨Φ|Ψ⟩|2
                                                          ,                                    (S.21)
                                               ⟨Φ|Φ⟩⟨Ψ|Ψ⟩
     which is independent of the overall normalization of both |Φ⟩ and |Ψ⟩.
 (ii) Consider any state with a state vector of the form α |1⟩ + β |2⟩ with α ̸= 0. In that
      case, the vector |1⟩ + (β/α) |2⟩ represents the same state (since it only differs from
      α |1⟩ + β |2⟩ by a scalar factor), and is in the form |1⟩ + z |2⟩. Therefore, any such
      state can be represented by a vector of the form |1⟩ + z |2⟩.
     The only state that cannot be represented by vector of the form α |1⟩ + β |2⟩ with
     α ̸= 0 is the the one that corresponds to vectors µ |2⟩, µ ̸= 0. Hence there is only
     one state which cannot be represented by a vector of the form |1⟩ + z |2⟩.
     If z ̸= z ′ , |1⟩ + z |2⟩ and |1⟩ + z ′ |2⟩ are not scalar multiples of each other, and hence
     would not represent the same state. For example, a measurement operator with
     |1⟩ + z |2⟩ as a non-degenerate eigenstate with eigenvalue λ would have probability
     1 of measuring the outcome λ when applied to |1⟩+z |2⟩, but would have probability
     < 1 of measuring the outcome λ when applied to |1⟩ + z ′ |2⟩.
(iii) In this limit, |1⟩ + z |2⟩ becomes closer and closer to simply z |2⟩, so one can think
      of that limit as one that brings us to the state represented by vectors proportional
      to |2⟩.
(iv) Parts (ii)–(iii) yield a mapping from the extended complex plane to physically in-
     equivalent states, given by z → |1⟩ + z |2⟩ for z ∈ C and ∞ → |2⟩. This mapping is
     bijective (noting that two states are distinct if and only if their state vectors are not
     scalar multiples of each other), and so the set of states is in bijective correspondence
     with the extended complex plane C ∪ {∞}. The latter is topologically equivalent
     (‘homeomorphic’) to a two-dimensional sphere, sometimes known as a Bloch sphere.
