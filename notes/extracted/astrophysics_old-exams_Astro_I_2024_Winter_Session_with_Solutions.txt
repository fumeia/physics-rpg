2. (3 points) Consider two stars, S1 and S2 with B-V colors of 0.5 and 1.0 respectively.
   Which of the following statements has to be true about these stars over the range of
   wavelengths these bands cover? Note that the B band is bluer than the V band.
         ■ S1 emits more short wavelength light than S2.
         □ S2 emits more short wavelength light than S1.
         □ S1 must be brighter than S2.
         □ S2 must be more distant than S1.


    Solution: S1 has a smaller B-V color, implying it is relatively brighter in the B band
    than the V band. Since the B band is bluer than the V band, S1 emits more short
    wavelength light than S2. Distance and brightness differences cannot be inferred by
    difference in colors alone.
3. (3 points) A system of size L with collisional mean free path l can be said to be in
   local thermal equilibrium through collisions but not in global thermal equilibrium if
   macroscopic thermodynamic variables (e.g. T and P ) are
         □ constant and l ≫ L.
         □ constant and l ≪ L.
         □ depend on time and/or location and l ≫ L.

14. (3 points) In the history of our universe, different contributions to the overall energy
    density dominate the dynamics of the expansion in different epochs. In our universe:
           ■ Radiation dominates over matter at early times, then matter dominates over
             radiation at late times.
           □ Matter dominates over radiation at early times, then radiation dominates over
             matter at late times.
           □ Radiation always dominates over matter.
           □ Matter always dominates over radiation.


     Solution: In the history of our universe, at early times before matter-radiation
     equality (∼50,000 years after the big bang), radiation dominated over matter. Since
     then, matter has dominated over radiation (and dark energy has become relevant
     at very late times). This is due to the fact that the energy density of radiation,
     ρR ∝ a−4 and that of matter, ρM ∝ a−3 .

5. (6 points) Briefly explain three differences in the properties of spiral and elliptical galax-
   ies, describing how each property is exhibited in both types of galaxies.


     Solution: Differences between spiral and elliptical galaxies include (only 3 needed):

        • Morphology: ellipticals have smooth elliptical shapes, spirals have disks with
          spiral arms and a bulge and sometimes a bar

        • Internal dynamics: ellipticals are supported by random motion, while the disk
          of spirals is supported by circular motion

        • Color: Elliptical are redder, while spirals are bluer

        • Stellar population: ellipticals have little star formation and thus have old stel-
          lar populations, while spirals have active star formation and a young stellar
          populations

        • Gas content: Ellipticals have little cold gas, while spirals has high quantities
          of cold gas

        • Clustering: Ellipticals are more clustered (higher bias), than spirals (lower
          bias)

        • Environment: ellipticals are more abundant in galaxy clusters, while spirals
          are more abundant in the field (low density regions)

     Other differences include surface brightness distribution, intensity profiles and lumi-
     nosity functions.
