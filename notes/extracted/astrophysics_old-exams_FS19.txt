Prof: Mhm, this is not really correct. Our sun could at best become a degenerate gas. No idea if I
just remembered the plot incorrectly or if I just misunderstood it at this point. But lets move on. Can
you explain to me what a degenerate gas is?
Me: Yes. The degenerate gas is a gas that is really high in density and usually rather low on the
temperature side. It is relevant for us since it is the main stabilizing mechanism for stars that don’t
have nuclear burning anymore. Let me explain. If a star exhausts its Hydrogen the gravitational pull
inwards wont be balanced anymore by the radiation, so the star starts collapsing. The density and core
temperature therefore start to rise. At a certain density the electron degeneracy pressure will start to
kick in and the star will be balanced. If the star is too massive this might not be enough to balance
gravity and the star collapses further. If the density rises sufficiently high neutron degeneracy pressure
will kick in and start stabilizing our core.
