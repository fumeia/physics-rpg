Prof: Okay, let’s move on. What can you tell me about galaxy clusters?
Me: Galaxy clusters contain galaxies that are gravitationally bound and where gravity is balanced
by random motion. We typically find more ellipticals, up to 80-90 %, than spirals, whereas isolated
galaxies have a higher fraction of spirals. We believe this is due to collisions of galaxies where when
two spirals collide we get an elliptical. Due to these effects and galactic cannibalism we get a higher
fraction of ellipticals.
Prof: Yes, now how many galaxies are there in a cluster?
Me: Well, our Galaxy is in the local group which has 35 galaxies, so typically about 100.
Prof: Yes, can be up to 1000. Now what are the typical masses?
Me: We have two different mass estimates. From considerations of the luminosity we get 1013 M .

Prof: It is called the hydrostatic equation. How did we derive the structure equations?
Me: For equation 2, we considered a thin spherical shell of the star and the mass inside given a certain
density. For equation 3, we looked at the pressures on the inside and outside of that shell. The difference
should be balanced by gravity. For equation 4, we considered the addition to the luminosity coming
from the spherical shell, which comes from the nuclear reactions taking place in the star. We derived
equation 5 using the radiative transfer equations. For equation 6 we considered hot blobs of gas rising
to the surface thus constituting the convective heat flow.

Prof: So, can we solve these equations with the boundary conditions?
Me: Analytically no, unless we make some major simplifications. But we can solve them numerically.
Prof: So what variables must be specified before we can solve?
Me: χ and  must be given as functions of T , Xi and ρ. We also need an equation to relate the pressure
to the density.
Prof: What do we call this equation?
Me: The state equation...
Prof: The equation of state, yes. Okay, so how do χ and  change with time?
Me: Slowly, so we can first solve with those values fixed and then change them a bit after some time
and solve the equations again.
Prof: Yes, we can iterate and update our stellar model discretely. For which stars do we have radiative
heat transfer?
Me: In smaller stars like our sun, we have radiative transfer on the inside and convective transfer on
the outside. It is the other way around for larger stars.
