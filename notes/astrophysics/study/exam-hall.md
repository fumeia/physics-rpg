## 📖 How the Refregier exam works (and how to use this hall) (intuition)

Format (identical in all four recent exams): <b>15 multiple-choice questions × 3 pt = 45 pt</b> and <b>7 open questions ≈ 64–68 pt</b>, total ≈ 110 pt. Allowed: 4 A4 pages of notes (your formula codex), a non-programmable calculator, the constants table (G, k_B, h, c, m_p, M☉, R☉, pc, Jy, σ_T). Write down intermediate steps.

The open questions are always: one photometry/angles question, one radiation (blackbody/CMB) question, one stellar-structure or Eddington question, one or two galaxy dynamics questions (virial, rotation curves, tidal, Plummer), one AGN/cluster question, and a Friedmann question at the end.

📝 **How the exam asks it.** Mock-exam routine: do one MC set in 30 minutes without hints, then 2-3 open questions (bosses) under their timers. Two official keys contain arithmetic errors (HS24 summer Q2b: 1.77 pW, not 2.5 pW; HS25 practice Q6c: $2.2\times10^{11}M_\odot$, not $9.8\times10^{11}$): trust your own calculation.

## 👹 Exam boss: thermal bremsstrahlung and the cluster L_X–M relation

*HS25 winter: Open Question 5 [11 pt], 20 min.*

Consider the total X-ray luminosity due to thermal bremsstrahlung from the ionised intracluster plasma at temperature $T$. We derive an approximate scaling relation between this luminosity and the total cluster mass.

(a) [3 pt] Given $\epsilon_\nu\propto n_en_pT^{-1/2}e^{-h\nu/k_BT}$ (Gaunt factor constant), show $L_X=\int dV\int_0^\infty\epsilon_\nu d\nu\propto\int\rho_g^2T^{1/2}dV$. Use $n_e=n_p$ and $\rho_g=\mu_en_em_p$ with constant $\mu_e$.

(b) [3 pt] Assuming virial equilibrium with thermal support, derive a scaling relation for $T$ in terms of $M$ and $\langle R\rangle$. Take $\bar V\propto-GM^2/\langle R\rangle$, kinetic energy per particle $\propto k_BT$, $N$ particles of mass $\mu_em_p$ making up $M_g=f_gM$.

(c) [2 pt] Show that with $M\propto\langle R\rangle^3$, $T\propto M^{2/3}$.

(d) [3 pt] Approximating $L_X\propto\rho_g^2T^{1/2}\langle R\rangle^3$, derive $L_X\propto M^{4/3}$.

<details><summary>Key points of a full-marks solution</summary>

- (a) $\int_0^\infty e^{-h\nu/k_BT}d\nu=k_BT/h$, so $\int\epsilon_\nu d\nu\propto n_e^2T^{1/2}\propto\rho_g^2T^{1/2}$.
- (b) $\bar K\propto Nk_BT=\frac{f_gM}{\mu_em_p}k_BT\propto MT$; $\bar K\propto-\bar V$ gives $T\propto M/\langle R\rangle$.
- (c)-(d) $\langle R\rangle\propto M^{1/3}\Rightarrow T\propto M^{2/3}$; $\rho_g\propto M/\langle R\rangle^3$ = const, so $L_X\propto M^{1/3}\cdot M=M^{4/3}$.

</details>

## 👹 Exam boss: black-hole growth at the Eddington limit

*HS25 winter: Open Question 6 [8 pt], 15 min.*

A black hole of mass $M_{BH}(t)$ accretes at rate $\dot M_{\rm acc}$. A fraction $\eta=0.1$ of the rest-mass energy is radiated, $L=\eta\dot M_{\rm acc}c^2$; the rest is added to the black hole. Assume it radiates at the Eddington luminosity $L_{Edd}=4\pi GM_{BH}m_pc/\sigma_T$.

(a) [6 pt] Write a differential equation for $M_{BH}(t)$ in terms of $\eta$, $L_{Edd}$ and constants. Substitute $L_{Edd}$ and show $M_{BH}(t)=M_{BH}(0)e^{t/t_S}$. Give the Salpeter time $t_S$ and show $t_S\simeq5.0\times10^7$ yr.

(b) [2 pt] Estimate the time for the black hole to double its mass.

<details><summary>Key points of a full-marks solution</summary>

- $\dot M_{\rm acc}=L_{Edd}/\eta c^2$, $\dot M_{BH}=(1-\eta)\dot M_{\rm acc}=\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}\frac{4\pi Gm_p}{\sigma_Tc}M_{BH}$.
- Exponential solution with $t_S=\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}\frac{c\sigma_T}{4\pi Gm_p}=5.0\times10^7$ yr.
- $t_{dbl}=t_S\ln2=3.5\times10^7$ yr.

</details>

## 👹 Exam boss: tidal disruption of galaxies

*HS24 summer: Open Question 6 [10 pt], 15 min.*

Consider a galaxy of mass $m$ and radius $r$ at distance $d$ from a more massive galaxy of mass $M$.

(a) [4 pt] The tidal acceleration is $a_{\rm tidal}=a_{\rm grav}(d)-a_{\rm grav}(d+r)$. Show $a_{\rm tidal}\simeq2GMr/d^3$ for $r\ll d$.

(b) [3 pt] The small galaxy is disrupted if its surface acceleration $a_g\simeq Gm/r^2$ is smaller than the tidal acceleration. Show that they are equal at $d_{\rm limit}=(2M/m)^{1/3}r$.

(c) [3 pt] How close can a Milky-Way-like spiral ($m\approx10^{12}M_\odot$, $r\approx10$ kpc) come to a cD galaxy ($M\approx10^{14}M_\odot$) without being disrupted? Answer in kpc.

<details><summary>Key points of a full-marks solution</summary>

- (a) $GM[d^{-2}-(d+r)^{-2}]=\frac{GM}{d^2}[1-(1+r/d)^{-2}]\simeq\frac{GM}{d^2}\frac{2r}{d}$.
- (b) $2GMr/d^3=Gm/r^2\Rightarrow d=(2M/m)^{1/3}r$.
- (c) $200^{1/3}\times10$ kpc $=58.5$ kpc.

</details>

## 👹 Exam boss: extinction towards the Galactic centre

*HS24 summer: Open Question 4 [12 pt], 25 min.*

Visual extinction is taken proportional to the hydrogen column, $A_V\propto N_H=\int_0^\infty n_H\,ds$, along the line of sight to an extragalactic object at infinity. The Sun is at $d=8$ kpc from the Galactic centre; the line of sight has latitude $b$ at $l=0^\circ$.

(a) [8 pt] Using the cosine rule, find the distance $r(s)$ from the Galactic centre of a point at distance $s$ along the line of sight. For $n_H\propto\exp(-(r/r_0)^2)$ and $A_V(b=0)\approx30$, show $A_V(b)=30\exp\left[\frac{d^2}{r_0^2}(\cos^2b-1)\right]$ (use $\int e^{-(ax^2+bx+c)}dx\approx\sqrt{\pi/a}\,e^{b^2/4a-c}$).

(b) [4 pt] Compute $A_V$ and the flux ratio with/without extinction for $l=0^\circ$, $b=15^\circ$, $r_0=1.2$ kpc.

<details><summary>Key points of a full-marks solution</summary>

- $r(s)=\sqrt{d^2+s^2-2ds\cos b}$.
- Exponent $-\frac{1}{r_0^2}(s^2-2ds\cos b+d^2)$; Gaussian integral gives $N_H\propto r_0\sqrt\pi\exp[\frac{d^2}{r_0^2}(\cos^2b-1)]$; ratio to $b=0$ gives the formula.
- $A_V=1.53$, $F_{\rm ext}/F_0=10^{-0.61}=0.24$.

</details>

## 👹 Exam boss: virial theorem with the harmonic radius

*HS24 summer: Open Question 5 [9 pt], 15 min.*

A collisionless self-gravitating system of total mass $M$ and $N$ particles of identical mass $m$ is in virial equilibrium.

(a) [3 pt] Show the total kinetic energy is $K=\frac12M\sigma_v^2$ with $\sigma_v^2=\frac1N\sum v_i^2$.

(b) [3 pt] Show that for large $N$, $V\simeq-GM^2/(2r_H)$, where $r_H^{-1}=\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\sum_{i>j}r_{ij}^{-1}$.

(c) [3 pt] Show $\sigma_v^2=GM/(2r_H)$.

<details><summary>Key points of a full-marks solution</summary>

- (a) $K=\sum\frac12mv_i^2=\frac12mN\sigma_v^2=\frac12M\sigma_v^2$.
- (b) $V=-\sum_{i>j}\frac{Gm^2}{r_{ij}}=-Gm^2\frac{N(N-1)}{2}r_H^{-1}\simeq-\frac{GM^2}{2r_H}$.
- (c) $2K+V=0\Rightarrow\sigma_v^2=GM/(2r_H)$.

</details>

## 👹 Exam boss: the Milky Way's escape velocity and mass

*HS25 practice: Open Question 6 [15 pt], 25 min.*

In the solar neighbourhood the Milky Way has a flat rotation curve $v(r)=v_c$, implying $\rho\propto r^{-2}$.

(a) [3 pt] For a flat rotation curve with a cut-off radius $R$ beyond which the density is zero, derive $M_r$.

(b) [6 pt] Show that the escape velocity from any $r<R$ is $v_{esc}^2=2v_c^2(1+\ln(R/r))$, starting from $\frac12mv_{esc}^2\equiv-\int_\infty^r\frac{GM_{r'}m}{r'^2}dr'$.

(c) [6 pt] The largest velocity of any star near $r\approx8$ kpc is 440 km/s. Assuming it is bound, find lower limits on $R$ (kpc) and on the mass of the galaxy ($M_\odot$). Take $v_c=230$ km/s.

<details><summary>Key points of a full-marks solution</summary>

- (a) $M_r=Mr/R$ ($r<R$), $M$ ($r\ge R$).
- (b) Split the integral at $R$: $\frac{GM}{R}(1+\ln\frac Rr)$, and $v_c^2=GM/R$.
- (c) $R>8e^{(440/230)^2/2-1}=18.3$ kpc; $M=v_c^2R/G>2.2\times10^{11}M_\odot$ (the key's $9.8\times10^{11}$ is an arithmetic slip).

</details>
